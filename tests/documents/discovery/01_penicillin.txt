In 1928, Scottish bacteriologist Alexander Fleming returned from a two-week
vacation to discover that mold had contaminated his petri dish of Staphylococcus
bacteria. Rather than discarding the ruined experiment, Fleming noticed that the
bacteria surrounding the Penicillium mold had been killed. He identified the
mold as Penicillium notatum and found it secreted a bacteria-killing substance
he named penicillin. This accidental observation led to the development of the
world's first antibiotic, which has since saved countless millions of lives.
