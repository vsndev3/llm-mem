In 1938, chemist Roy Plunkett was working at DuPont on new refrigerant gases.
He stored a tank of tetrafluoroethylene gas at freezing temperature but when he
returned to use it, the gas had mysteriously disappeared — leaving a slippery
white powder in its place. Instead of discarding the failed experiment, Plunkett
investigated the polymerized residue and found it was heat-resistant, chemically
inert, and extraordinarily slippery. This accidental discovery became Teflon
(polytetrafluoroethylene), now used worldwide in non-stick cookware and
industrial coatings.
