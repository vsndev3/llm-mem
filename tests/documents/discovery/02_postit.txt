In 1968, 3M scientist Spencer Silver was trying to develop a super-strong
adhesive for the aerospace industry. Instead, he accidentally created a weak,
pressure-sensitive adhesive that could stick to surfaces but peeled off cleanly
without residue. The discovery was considered useless until 1974, when his
colleague Art Fry wanted a bookmark that would not fall out of his church hymn
book or damage its pages. The combination of Silver's failed adhesive and Fry's
need produced the Post-it Note, which became a ubiquitous office product
generating billions of dollars in revenue worldwide.
