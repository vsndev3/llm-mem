In 1941, Swiss electrical engineer George de Mestral returned from a hunting
walk with his dog in the Alps and noticed burdock burrs stubbornly clinging to
his wool trousers and his dog's fur. Instead of just removing them, he became
curious about their extraordinary sticking power. Examining the burrs under a
microscope, de Mestral saw hundreds of tiny hooks that caught on anything with
loops. He spent years replicating this natural mechanism synthetically,
eventually patenting Velcro, a hook-and-loop fastener now used in shoes,
clothing, medical devices, and even NASA spacecraft.
