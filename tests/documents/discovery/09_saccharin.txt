In 1879, chemist Constantin Fahlberg was working with coal tar derivatives at
Johns Hopkins University. One evening at dinner he noticed his bread tasted
unusually sweet, tracing it back to a chemical compound he had spilled on his
hands earlier in the laboratory — benzoic sulfimide. Rather than washing his
hands before eating, Fahlberg had carried the substance to his meal, leading
to the discovery of saccharin, the world's first artificial sweetener. This
accidental contamination revealed a compound approximately 300 times sweeter
than sugar, creating a multibillion-dollar industry.
