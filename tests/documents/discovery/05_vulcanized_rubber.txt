In 1839, Charles Goodyear had spent years obsessively trying to make natural
rubber stable and commercially useful. Raw rubber became sticky in summer heat
and brittle in winter cold, making it impractical for any serious application.
One day Goodyear accidentally dropped a mixture of rubber and sulfur powder onto
a hot stove. Instead of melting into a useless goo, the rubber charred slightly
but remained firm, elastic, and resistant to temperature changes. Goodyear
named this process vulcanization, which made rubber suitable for tires, hoses,
footwear, and industrial seals, revolutionizing both transportation and
manufacturing.
