In 1895, German physicist Wilhelm Röntgen was experimenting with cathode rays in
a darkened laboratory when he noticed a fluorescent screen across the room
glowing unexpectedly. Investigating further, he discovered invisible rays that
could pass through solid objects and produce images of bones on photographic
plates. Röntgen named them X-rays. This accidental observation during unrelated
physics experiments gave birth to medical imaging, earning him the first Nobel
Prize in Physics in 1901.
